Bad Money / Good Money

A cross-functional discovery session that untangled one of payroll's most confusing edge cases — negative and retroactive taxable earnings — and aligned engineers, LPMs, and designers on the expected behavior and a phased plan to deliver it.

My role
UX Designer & facilitator
Format
Discovery workshop (FigJam)
With
Developers, LPMs, UX designers
Focus
Systems thinking, scenario mapping, alignment

Overview

"Bad money" is how the team refers to payroll scenarios that produce negative or retroactive taxable earnings — an overpayment clawed back after a retroactive salary change, statutory deductions calculated on money the employee never actually received, a negative "to be paid" that reads like the employee owes the company. These are rare, high-stakes cases where the numbers stop being intuitive.

I facilitated a discovery session with developers, Lead Product Managers (LPMs), and UX designers to walk through a concrete scenario together, capture every open question, agree on the expected behavior, and break the work into deliverable phases. The board below is the artifact we built live during the session.

The challenge

Payroll is a domain where a single edge case can ripple across tax, arrears, and net pay. The "bad money" cases were understood differently by each discipline, so before any UI could be designed we needed a shared mental model: what the system should do, where it currently misleads people, and which parts are design problems versus engineering rules. My job was to make that complexity visible and get the whole triad — dev, product, and design — aligned on the same picture.

The discovery board

We mapped the problem on a single FigJam board: the participants, the use cases in scope, a step-by-step walkthrough of a real scenario, the questions and answers raised around it, and the phased plan we landed on.

Use cases in scope

The negative taxable-earnings scenarios we needed the design and the rules to cover.

Use case 1Negative taxable earnings in the current period due to a retroactive overpayment that exceeds current-period taxable earnings, resulting in a negative net (overpayment) scenario.
Use case 2Negative taxable earnings in the current period forwarded from a prior overpayment that has not yet been cleared.
Use case 3Negative taxable earnings due to a retroactive change to a non-cash (not included in gross or net pay) taxable earning that exceeds current-period taxable earnings.
Use case 4Negative taxable earnings due to a negative amount entered for a taxable earning.
Use case 5Negative taxable earnings due to a negative amount entered for a taxable non-cash earning.
Use case 6Negative taxable earnings due to a retroactive change to a taxable earning that creates a negative net, where the offset/clearing wage type does not carry the correct taxability of the original payment.

Questions, answers & ahas

Question / risk Agreed behavior Expected behavior / insight
Questions & risks
A negative "to be paid" gives the impression the EE has to pay 6k USD. In payroll a negative result could be a refund — not true for this scenario either.
Statutory deductions on the 10k base salary were already paid — shouldn't the next pay periods be tax-free, or arrears be generated for statutory deductions?A: taxes aren't processed until the "to be paid" is positive.
The OTP is treated as a supplemental payment, but this case likely needs a dedicated PCT (payroll calculation type).
With a high pre-tax amount, how will it be calculated across regular and supplemental payments?
Will there be UX deliverables for Phase 1 — an IPS footnote, an alert on the payroll dashboard? Any documentation for manually solving the issue?
Agreed behavior
Taxes shouldn't be processed while the "to be paid" is negative — statutory deductions should be 0.
Overpayments take higher priority than voluntary deductions (pre-tax / post-tax benefits) and would generate arrears depending on how the deductions are configured.
Expected behavior & insights
Expected: "to be paid" should be the 2k USD base salary minus deductions based on the 2k salary.
Even with a negative net pay, the effective payout is 0 across all cases in the Employee Payments app.

Phased delivery of taxable-wage processing

Phase 1

Detect BSI errors and stop payroll with a clear alert.

Phase 2

Handle cases with no positive cash (postponement).

Phase 3

Process mixed positive and negative wages (retro amounts and period amounts together).

Good Money — the flip side

"Good money" is the opposite scenario: higher taxable amounts (bonuses, gifts). The employee receives a separate amount and the taxables increase because of it — a useful mirror for pressure-testing the same logic in the positive direction.

Outcome

The session turned a fuzzy, discipline-specific problem into a shared, phased plan. Engineers, product, and design left with the same mental model of how "bad money" should behave, a prioritized set of open questions, and a three-phase roadmap to build against.

Just as importantly, the board surfaced the design questions early — where an alert, an Interactive Pay Statement footnote, or supporting documentation would be needed — so UX work could start from a place of clarity instead of guesswork.
