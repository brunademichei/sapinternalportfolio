Product design · Journey mapping

Minha Jornada Platform

A centralized journey hub for AIESEC exchange participants and managers in Brazil, turning a fragmented operational process into a guided, transparent, end-to-end experience.

My role
UX/UI design, journey mapping, IA
Context
AIESEC in Brazil
Users
Exchange participants & managers
Deliverables
Flows, wireframes, high-fidelity UI

Use case

Minha Jornada was conceived as a shared platform for exchange participants (EPs) and their managers to understand, manage, and complete the AIESEC exchange journey in Brazil.

Objectives

Create one guided journey

  • Give participants clarity about their current stage and next action.
  • Help managers monitor progress, engagement, and compliance.
  • Establish a shared source of truth across the exchange lifecycle.
Pain points

Fragmented information

  • Instructions and updates were spread across spreadsheets, chats, and other tools.
  • Participants lacked visibility into progress and ownership of next steps.
  • Managers relied on manual follow-up to understand each participant's status.
Current experience

Reactive and inconsistent

  • Participants pieced together information and asked managers for clarification.
  • Managers reconciled multiple sources before taking action.
  • Both roles navigated the same process without a consistent progress model.

Analyzing the current experience

I mapped how information moved between participants, managers, spreadsheets, and communication channels. The analysis showed that the experience was not failing at a single touchpoint: the main issue was the absence of a connected journey.

Each update created another moment of coordination. Participants needed to discover where information lived, confirm what applied to them, and wait for guidance. Managers then had to consolidate updates manually before they could identify who needed support.

  1. 1 Find information Search across disconnected channels and files.
  2. 2 Ask for clarity Confirm the current stage and expected action.
  3. 3 Complete a task Act without a single end-to-end view.
  4. 4 Share an update Report progress through chat or a spreadsheet.
  5. 5 Reconcile status Managers consolidate sources and follow up.

The available project artifacts focused on understanding the operational journey and translating it into a product structure. They created the foundation for the role-based experience and its information architecture.

Proposed ideal experience

The proposed flow brings both roles into one platform, then adapts the experience according to their responsibilities. Participants receive guided next steps, while managers see the complete pipeline and the items requiring attention.

  1. 1 Enter the platform Understand the value of Minha Jornada.
  2. 2 Select a role Choose participant or manager.
  3. 3 Create an account Provide only the information needed to start.
  4. 4 See a tailored dashboard Access role-specific status, tasks, and priorities.
  5. 5 Act on the next step Move forward with clear guidance and ownership.
  6. 6 Track shared progress Keep the journey visible in one place.

Low-fidelity prototypes

I explored the structure in low fidelity before defining visual details. The wireframes tested the content hierarchy, entry points, and early role split while keeping iteration fast.

High-fidelity prototype

The high-fidelity direction turns the journey model into a welcoming participant experience and a focused operational cockpit for managers. The longer screens scroll inside their frames.

Takeaways

The strongest opportunity was not another isolated tool, but a shared model of the journey. Centralizing progress, ownership, and next steps creates a more supportive experience for participants and a more actionable view for managers.

  • Design the journey before the screens: mapping stages and handoffs exposed the systemic problem.
  • Use one progress model, adapt the interface: both roles need shared status but different actions.
  • Structure before styling: low-fidelity work clarified hierarchy and navigation early.
  • Next step: validate the flow with participants and managers, then refine language, priorities, and edge cases.
